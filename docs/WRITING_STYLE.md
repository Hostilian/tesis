# Writing style

The reader is a supervisor or examiner with little time. Every sentence should either tell them something true and specific or help them find it.

## Rules

1. **Say the thing.** Lead with the finding, number or decision, then the reason.
   - Don't: "It is important to note that the results demonstrate a significant pattern."
   - Do: "Summer NDVI rose on 2,260 ha (22 % of the area) between 2018 and 2025."
2. **Use numbers with units and a source.** "0.86 % of control pixels (`results.json`, `change.control_flagged_share_at_threshold`)", not "a low false-alarm rate".
3. **Short sentences, common words.** Prefer *use* to *utilize*, *show* to *demonstrate*, *about* to *approximately*. Aim for about 20 words per sentence, and break up anything over 30.
4. **Claim only what the evidence supports.** Use *shows* for measurements, *suggests* or *is consistent with* for interpretation, *cannot tell* for limits. Say what is not known.
5. **No filler or hype.** Avoid these words and phrases: *revolutionize, unprecedented, cutting-edge, seamless, robust (as praise), leverage, delve, landscape, realm, holistic, synergy, game-changer, premium, enterprise-grade, plays a crucial role, it is worth noting, in today's world*.
6. **No stacked qualifiers or triplets for rhythm.** "fast, scalable and reliable" means nothing without evidence for each word.
7. **Active voice, first person where it is natural.** "I compared…" or "The pipeline reads…", not "It was decided that…".
8. **One idea per paragraph.** Open with the point; follow with the evidence.
9. **Define each term once and keep it.** "Candidate change" always means the thresholded flag, so don't vary it to "anomaly", "detection" or "hit".
10. **Formatting serves reading.** Use tables for comparisons, numbered lists for steps and figures with full captions. Use bold sparingly. No emoji in the thesis.

## Thesis-specific vocabulary

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| candidate change | anomaly (unless describing the old demo) |
| agreement with COS | accuracy, ground truth |
| control pair (short-interval comparison) | no-change truth |
| observation / derived result / interpretation | verified (unless it is) |
| Sentinel-2 L2A surface reflectance | raw satellite data |

## Emails

- A subject line that states the topic.
- Two to four short lines of context.
- Numbered questions, each answerable in one sentence.
- Links instead of attachments where possible.
- No apologies, no flattery.

## AI-use boundary (CZU Directive 7/2026, Art. 8)

AI may reword and polish the literature review. For results, discussion and conclusions it supplies facts and tables only; the author writes the prose. Mark AI-assisted text so it can be declared.
